Wing geometry and kinematic parameters optimization of bat-like robot fixed-altitude flight for minimum energy

Bosong Duan, Chuangqiang Guo, Tianyou Mao, Hong Liu

Aerospace Science and Technology, 2023
Publisher PDF

Overview

This paper jointly optimizes wing geometry and flapping kinematics to reduce the power required for fixed-altitude flight of a bat-like robot. It combines deformable-wing dynamics, a power-density objective, and constrained numerical optimization to move beyond direct imitation of biological wing parameters.

Main contributions

  1. Deformable-wing dynamics and power modeling

    A revised quasi-steady model accounts for active wing folding, passive membrane deformation, rotational circulation, aerodynamic damping, and wing-structure gravity and inertia. Its aerodynamic predictions are checked against XFlow simulations before the model is used to estimate flapping power.

  2. Joint geometry and kinematics optimization

    A hybrid genetic–particle swarm algorithm jointly selects wing-segment lengths, flapping amplitude, and frequency. The power-density objective is constrained by lift-to-weight ratio, Reynolds number, aspect ratio, Strouhal number, and parameter bounds to target fixed-altitude flight.

  3. Lower predicted power at the optimized operating point

    In the reported model comparison, power density decreases from 50.8831 to 24.9841 W/kg, a reduction of about 51%. The optimized flapping frequency falls from 7 to 5 Hz, while the lift-to-weight ratio approaches one.

  4. Sensitivity analysis for wing design

    One-parameter-at-a-time studies identify trade-offs between lift support and power demand. Within the tested ranges, increasing wing dimensions raises both quantities, while flapping frequency has a strongly nonlinear effect on power density, helping prioritize geometry and motion parameters.

Summary based on the published paper, Sections 2–6. Results come from analytical modeling, CFD comparison, and numerical optimization.
